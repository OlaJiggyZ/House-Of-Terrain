HOW TO SMELL · THE LANGUAGE OF SCENT

The nose learns
what the eye
already knows.

Vision has a vocabulary that is centuries old. Scent does not. House of Terrain uses a four-part framework to document every material — because the language you bring to an encounter with a raw material determines what you are able to find there.

I

First
contact.

Top note · 0 – 30 minutes

The volatile molecules that reach the nose immediately — evanescent, often misleading. This is the material at its most defensive. It has not yet had time to open, to warm, to become itself. A nose trained to judge at first contact will miss most of what a material has to offer. The first register is recorded, but it is not the verdict.

FROM THE ARCHIVE — HYRACEUM First contact: animalic, leathery, faintly urinal. Sharper than expected. The material presents its least accommodating face. Hold.
II

Warmed
wet.

Heart note · 30 minutes – 4 hours

The material after skin chemistry and ambient heat have had time to work on it. This is the most honest register — the full-bodied, most complex phase, where the character of the raw ingredient is revealed rather than concealed. Great materials open here into something that could not have been predicted from first contact. The heart note is what the perfumer composes around.

FROM THE ARCHIVE — HYRACEUM Warmed: tobacco, leather, dried fruit — the animalic quality softening into something almost aristocratic. A material of extraordinary depth. The patience was correct.
III

Memory.

Base note · 4 – 24 hours

The involuntary image or place the scent calls forward, unprompted. Not a metaphor chosen by the writer — a literal memory retrieved by the oldest circuitry in the brain. The limbic system processes scent before the cortex has the opportunity to intervene with language. The memory that arises is the truest register. It is what the base note leaves behind in the mind long after the material has faded from the skin.

FROM THE ARCHIVE — LATERITE SOIL AT NOON Memory: the ground before rain. A particular quality of stillness. The smell of a specific afternoon that had no occasion and no name.
IV

Emotional
register.

Sillage · the wake left behind

What the material produces in the body, not the nose. The quality of feeling — not named emotion, but the particular state the material calls forward. This is the register that separates perfumery from cosmetics. A great fragrance does not smell pleasant. It produces a condition — a readiness, a stillness, a specific quality of being present. The sillage is the trace this condition leaves in a room after its wearer has left.

FROM THE ARCHIVE — GRAINS OF SELIM Emotional register: communal warmth. The particular quality of a room where something important has been prepared. Grounding without weight.
THE H.O.T. FRAMEWORK · HOUSE OF TERRAIN
TERRAIN MATERIAL

The singular signature

Every accord House of Terrain builds is anchored by a single raw material sourced from a specific terrain — one that cannot be found elsewhere, substituted, or synthesised without loss of character. This is the Terrain Material. It gives the accord its irreplaceable identity. The four registers above are used to document it before a single gram of anything else is weighed.

THE QUESTION

The accord as answer

Before composition begins, the terrain poses a question. What does time smell like? What does the ground hold that the air does not? What is the scent of knowledge passed through hands for centuries? The accord is not complete until it answers this question — without stating it, without explaining it. The question is for the perfumer. The answer is for whoever smells the finished work.

IN PRACTICE

Approaching
a material for
the first time.

01

Apply to the inner wrist or inner elbow — not the back of the hand, where the skin is too dry and too exposed to ambient air.

02

Wait ten seconds before the first inhale. The initial volatile molecules need a moment to settle. What you smell in the first two seconds is not the material.

03

Do not rub. Friction accelerates evaporation and collapses the material's development before it can begin. The skin is the medium. Let it work.

04

Note the first contact without deciding. Write a single phrase. Then put the material aside for thirty minutes and return to it.

05

At thirty minutes, note the heart. This is the material's character. Compare it to the first contact note. The difference between them is the material's range.

06

Do not sample more than two materials at a time. The nose adapts quickly to complexity and begins to confuse what it cannot distinguish. Two is enough for one session.

07

The memory register arrives without effort — an image, a place, an afternoon. When it comes, write it exactly as it appeared, without editing it toward poetry. The precision is the point.
